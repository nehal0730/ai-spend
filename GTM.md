# Go-To-Market

## Exact target user

The clearest target user is an engineering manager at a 15-40 person B2B SaaS company who owns a small team, gets asked about AI spend, and already has 3-6 overlapping tools in motion. They are the person finance emails when the bill spikes and the person the team asks before buying one more subscription.

## What they search right before they care

They usually Google things like “Cursor team pricing,” “Claude team vs ChatGPT team,” “how much are we overpaying for AI tools,” “seat utilization,” or “OpenAI business pricing.” They also scroll X threads about AI stack recommendations, founder posts about tooling, and vendor pricing pages when they are trying to justify a renewal.

## Where they hang out

The best early channels are not broad startup audiences. They are YC Bookface, Indie Hackers, r/SaaS, r/Entrepreneur, engineering-lead Slack groups, local founder groups, and X lists that follow founders who post about their stack, hiring, and margins. The users who care first are the ones already talking about spend discipline in private channels.

## How to get the first 100 users in 30 days with $0

1. Post 5 short teardown threads on X showing anonymized AI stack waste patterns.
2. DM 20 founders and engineering managers a day with one concrete observation, not a pitch.
3. Ask friends, alumni, and operator Slack groups for “one person who owns AI tooling cost.”
4. Turn every completed audit into a share link and ask the user to forward it to the person who owns budget.
5. Repost the best anonymous before/after examples in founder communities and ask for another stack to compare.

The first 100 users come from founder-led distribution, not SEO. The product is easy to explain if the user can see their own waste.

## The unfair channel

The unfair channel is the report itself. Most tools ask for trust before they earn it. This one produces a shareable artifact that a founder can forward to finance, ops, or the CTO in one click. That creates an internal distribution path Credex can use without buying attention first.

## What week 1 traction looks like

If this is working, week 1 should show around 20-30 completed audits, 8-12 share links created, 4-6 direct conversations from those shares, and 1-2 serious consulting or credit-purchase conversations. The signal is not raw traffic. It is whether people feel comfortable forwarding the result to someone else.

## Positioning

The message should stay narrow: this is a deterministic audit for AI spend waste, not a full procurement platform. If the channel mix gets broader later, fine. Early on, the product should speak to people who already know they have tool sprawl and want a defensible baseline fast.
