# Economics

## Unit economics

The product is cheap to serve because the core audit is deterministic. A completed audit mostly costs backend compute, a database write for the share snapshot, and occasional AI summary or email calls.

Per completed audit, the rough variable cost is about $0.01 to $0.03. If the summary falls back to deterministic text, it stays closer to the low end. That means the product can afford to be generous with free audits as long as abuse stays under control.

## What a converted lead is worth

For Credex, a qualified lead should be worth roughly $300 to $800 in expected gross profit. Here is the model:

- 100 audits completed
- 10 people request a consultation or email the report
- 3 of those convert into a paid call or credit purchase
- average first purchase value: $1,500
- gross margin on that offer: 75%

Expected gross profit per 100 audits = 3 × $1,500 × 75% = $3,375.
So expected gross profit per completed audit is about $33.75 before sales labor.

If the offer is smaller, the math still works if the conversion is high enough. A $500 purchase at 75% margin needs much higher volume, so the product should bias toward higher-intent consultation paths instead of tiny transactional sales.

## CAC by channel

Founder content and community posting can stay in the $20-$60 CAC range per qualified lead because the main cost is time. Direct outbound is usually $50-$120 per qualified lead once you count manual research and follow-up. Product Hunt is closer to $150-$300 per qualified lead because launch effort is spiky and not every visitor is the right buyer. SEO should trend down over time, but in month one it is really just founder time with a delayed payoff.

## Conversion math

For this to be profitable, the funnel needs to look like this:

- audit completed → consultation booked: 8%-15%
- consultation booked → credit purchase: 20%-35%
- audit completed → credit purchase: roughly 2%-5%

At a $1,500 average first purchase and 75% gross margin, even a 2% audit-to-purchase rate can work if CAC stays under $100 per qualified lead. The product is not trying to monetize every audit. It is trying to find the subset with enough pain to pay for help.

## What would have to be true for $1M ARR in 18 months

To reach $1M ARR, Credex needs either 167 customers at $6,000 ARR or 100 customers at $10,000 ARR.

If audit-to-paid conversion is 2.5%, then the business needs about 6,680 completed audits to produce 167 customers. Over 18 months, that is about 371 completed audits per month, or roughly 12 per day.

That is not crazy if distribution works, but it only works if the product keeps producing shareable, credible reports and the founder follows up quickly on high-savings leads. If follow-up is slow, conversion collapses and the math stops working.

## The economic lever

The main lever is not lowering serving cost. It is improving the ratio of completed audits to paid conversations. A small improvement in conversion is worth more than squeezing another cent out of infrastructure.

If the team can keep variable cost below $0.03 per audit, hold CAC under $100 on the best channels, and convert at least 2%-5% of completed audits into paid work, the economics are good enough to support early scale. The bottleneck is sales efficiency, not compute.
